- **The alert** is `Alert` (`components/alert.rs`), for an inline message about a page's state: an icon, then the words, its `children`, so a link or the daemon's own words (`lang="en"`, 3.1.2) can stand in them. Its tone is an `AlertTone` (`components/alert/rules.rs`, decided on the host), the shared vocabulary's `Primary` (information, the default: the vocabulary has no Info), `Success`, `Warning` or `Error`, each with a Lucide glyph of its own, `info` and the status badge's `circle-check`, `triangle-alert` and `circle-x`, and a word that names it. The icon is an `Icon` labelled with that word, an image a screen reader hears before the words, so no tone is told by its color alone (1.4.1, A2). `Secondary` is left out, since it says no tone and has no icon of its own, and `Inherit`, since its icon and edge would take the text's color.
  - *Not a live region.* It has no `role="alert"` and no `aria-live`, unlike daisyUI's and Flowbite's alerts: a region that speaks whenever it is drawn speaks again each time a page draws it, and the page already has its announcer (A3). A failure that needs the reader now is said through the announcer's assertive region as well. An alert is never a stop for Tab; a link or a button in its words is.
  - *Dismissal.* A `Dismissal { on_dismiss, then_focus }` ends the alert with a Ghost Small icon-only `Button` (Lucide's `x`) named "Dismiss", its name shown by a `Tooltip` (`Tip::Names`). A press calls `on_dismiss`, and the page takes the alert away, so the page's own state says whether it shows and a later failure shows again. Focus on the button as the alert goes moves to the element with the ID `then_focus` (`hand_focus_when_gone`), so it never falls to the page (2.4.3); nothing is announced. The alert leaves through a reactive branch (a `move ||` closure or `Show`), never as an item of a keyed list, which takes the item off before the hand-off can see the focus.
  - *The look.* Tailwind utilities (decision 117), logical ones only, and no rule of its own in `style.css`: Ledger Gray (`bg-surface`) with the small corner, its words and any link in them in the text's color (`text-fg`), its tone in the icon and in a 0.25 rem edge at the start of its line (`border-s-[0.25rem]` in `--tone`), so a failure is never a red banner (DESIGN.md). The icon stands centered on the words' first line, and Dismiss at the line's end; the words wrap between words beside the icon (*Words wrap whole*, below). The contrast gate's pairs already cover it: each tone's token is 4.5:1 or more on `--surface`, as are `--fg` and the Ghost button's `--muted`, so it adds no token and no pair. Under forced colors a 1px `CanvasText` edge all round keeps its box in view, the start edge still 0.25 rem.
  - *Words wrap whole (#972, #1071).* The words and Dismiss share a box that takes all the room beside the icon (`flex-1`, `min-inline-size: 0`), so the words never go under the icon: a long path breaks beside it, where it once sent every word under it (#1071). In that box the words start as wide as their longest word (`flex-basis: min-content`), so where that doesn't fit beside Dismiss, Dismiss keeps to the end of a line of its own below them (`flex-wrap`). Only a word longer than the whole room beside the icon, a long path, breaks inside (`overflow-wrap: break-word`). `overflow-wrap: anywhere` counts a break between any two letters as the words' narrowest, so it kept Dismiss beside them and broke ordinary words there ("Ladderbac" over "k").

  The caller's duties under WCAG 2.2 AA: urgent text through the announcer's assertive region too (4.1.3); one alert for one failure, said once (DESIGN.md); words that say what happened and what to do (3.3.1, 3.3.3); a dismissed alert taken off the page, and `then_focus` kept on it.

  *A page's refusal (#929).* `components::refusal_alert` draws a refusal as an Error alert, the sender's words marked as English, and `say_refusals` says each refusal its signal comes to hold through the assertive region as the page's answer, once: a read refused again in the same words keeps its alert and says nothing new, and one cleared, then refused again, is said again. Inside a modal dialog the regions are inert, so `dialog_refusal_alert` puts the alert in a `role="alert"` of the dialog's own (the Dialog contract), and nothing else says it.

  The Log, Plans, Pending changes and Snapshots pages, and a snapshot's Restore and Download dialogs, draw their refusals this way; their features check that each is an Error alert in no live region, or in the dialog's, that the page's are said assertively and the dialog's by nothing else once it closes, and the refused Log page at 320 px, at 100 % text in light colors and at 200 % in dark. A refusal that a panel holds, such as the Snapshots page's maintenance and stats, is said through a signal derived from it, put into the words the page shows.

  The other pages followed (#929). Said assertively as the page's answer: the Start, Operations and Repositories pages' refusals, a row's Test and Create refusals (the row's own, which `refusal` puts into words), a refused cancel of a pending restart, the Full Disk Access steps' refusals, a refused save of Stay signed in for, a browser that kept no language or page width, and a failed sign-out, an Error alert at the top of the main content, not beside Sign out, so the header keeps its height. Settings' refused reads, of the API tokens, the dry-run allowlist and Signing in, are Error alerts in the list's place, said by nothing, as before.

  The restart, revoke and Sign out everywhere dialogs draw their refusals as the Plans page's delete dialog does; the Sign in again and idle warning dialogs keep their own `role="alert"` on the page from the dialog's start, an Error alert drawn inside it. A saved session length is a Success alert, still said politely. A field's own error, such as an API token's scopes under their group, stays the field's, as a text field's is. `features/ui/header.feature`, `operations.feature`, `repositories.feature`, `language.feature` and `page_width.feature`, `features/security/session_length.feature` and `admin_password.feature` check the alert's tone, no live region, and, where it is said, the assertive region.

  A forget preview's repositories that couldn't be read are a list in one answer, not a signal each, so the Pending changes page says each assertively as it draws the preview, which it does once per answer. The tree's own message, under it or in its place, is still its own: #929 moved the pages' messages, and the tree is a shared component.

  Taken from daisyUI's and Flowbite's alerts, the references: an icon per status and words beside it, information, success, warning and error, a close button at the end (Flowbite's dismissing alerts) and an edge on one side (Flowbite's border accent). Not taken: `role="alert"` on every alert (*Not a live region*, above), filled colored backgrounds (a red banner) and the soft, outline and dash styles, a title line, and a vertical layout at every width (the icon goes above the words only where they don't fit beside it).

  The gallery's "Alert" demo (`section#alert-demo`, an id that finds it under a pseudolocale too), after the Link demo and before the Text field demo, shows an alert in each tone, a link inside the warning, a long path that wraps inside the error, and a notice the reader dismisses, whose focus goes to the button that brings it back. `features/ui/components/alert.feature` checks axe and the ARIA snapshot, that no alert is a live region nor holds one, each icon's name and its one shape per tone, no stop for Tab, Enter and Space on Dismiss (gone, nothing said, focus handed on), the fill, words, icon and edge in light and dark colors, the edge and icon under forced colors, the edge, icon and Dismiss right to left too. At 320 px with 200 % text: the parts inside, words broken only between them, Dismiss at the end.
