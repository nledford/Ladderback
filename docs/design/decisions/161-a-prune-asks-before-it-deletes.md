# Decision 161: A prune asks the daemon before it deletes, and deletes nothing without a yes

**A prune asks the daemon at its first delete, with a `ready_to_delete` line on its stdout, whether it may delete. The daemon answers with one line on the prune's stdin, which stays open for it, after checking again, as at the run's start, that this machine may maintain the repository. The prune deletes, with the plan it worked out, only on `delete`. On `delete_nothing` it deletes nothing and fails with `maintenance-lease-lapsed`. On a closed stdin, a line that is no answer, or no answer within 60 seconds, it deletes nothing and fails with `maintenance-deletion-unanswered`.**

## Why

[Decision 151](151-a-paired-machine-maintains-only-under-its-hubs-lease.md) checks the lease when a run starts. A prune works out its plan, warms up the packs it repacks when they are on cold storage, repacks and writes its new index before it removes anything, and on a large repository or cold storage that takes hours. One started just before its lease ends could delete after the hub's hour of grace, when another machine may hold the repository (I1, #1482). The check at the first delete closes that gap where deletion begins.

- **At the first delete, not before the repack.** The question is asked by the prune's repository backend, at the first removal rustic makes, so the warm-up and the repack come before it, not between it and the deletion. Removals on other threads wait for the one answer.
- **The daemon decides, under the run's own lease.** The worker knows nothing of hubs or leases. It asks, and the daemon answers from `MaintenanceLeases::permits`, the check a run's start makes: a paired machine may while it holds a live lease on the hub's time, and one that isn't paired always may. The check must also find the lease the run started under: `permits` answers the lease's epoch, the run keeps it from its start, and a different epoch, or a machine paired or unpaired since, answers no. The hub grants a new epoch whenever no lease binds (§7.3), so the same epoch means the lease never lapsed past the hub's hour of grace, and no other machine held the repository meanwhile; a lease granted again may have let one, and the plan may be stale.
- **Checked, not renewed.** The question changes no lease, so a prune never lengthens the time a machine holds a repository.
- **The plan isn't worked out again.** On yes, the prune goes on with the plan it worked out. On no, it ends; the next prune, under a new lease, plans afresh.
- **Fail closed, and say why.** Anything but `delete` deletes nothing. Only `delete_nothing` says the lease lapsed: a missing, late or malformed answer, or a closed stdin, says it had no answer, since nothing then says a lease lapsed and the machine may not be paired at all. The worker reads one line of at most 64 bytes on a thread of its own and gives up after 60 seconds, so a daemon that stalls can't hold it.
- **On the event stream.** The question is an `EngineEvent` that carries its answer, not a method of the job's control. Every consumer that doesn't know it drops it, and a question dropped unanswered answers no, so only the maintenance run, which checks the lease, lets a prune delete. A run whose cancel comes before the answer is given, already there or while the lease is checked, answers no and stops the prune.
- **Only a prune asks, and only it reads the answer.** The daemon keeps stdin open for a prune alone, and fails a worker that asks outside a prune, or twice, as one that broke the protocol. The job spec is one line, so what follows it is the answer. The worker reads stdin through a close-on-exec copy and points descriptor 0 at `/dev/null`, as it does stdout, so `rclone` and any other program it starts neither takes the answer nor waits on the pipe.

## Consequences

- A prune whose lease lapsed while it ran fails with `maintenance-lease-lapsed`, its plan's work lost; it deletes nothing, so no other machine's backup loses a pack it needs.
- A prune told no has already repacked: the new packs and new index stay, beside the old index and packs, as a prune stopped at that point leaves them, and rustic's next prune takes the duplicates in. These writes add files and remove none, under the lease the run started with.
- A deletion already begun finishes: the answer is given once, at the first delete. The gap between the check and that delete is the time to write and read one line, and the daemon's check, done at once. I2's delayed deletion protects the packs the prune marks.
- The 60 seconds are on the monotonic clock, which stops while the computer sleeps. A daemon suspended between its check and its answer can still answer yes on waking, from a check made before the sleep. The window is the daemon's own answering, a few microseconds, and is accepted.
- The hub's ownership check reads a machine's approval outside the transaction that writes leases (found in #1439's review), so a machine removed at that moment can have its lease renewed once. Only one machine ever holds the lease, so I1 holds; the next owner waits one lease term longer. This decision leaves that window as it is: a prune's check reads the lease the machine holds, whatever granted it.
- The running-backup guard, the owner prune guard of §7.3's long-backup guard, still waits on the heartbeat feed (#573, #1463): this check stops a prune from outliving its lease, not from deleting packs a running backup still needs.
- An answer lost because the daemon stalled for over 60 seconds fails a prune that may have been allowed to delete, with `maintenance-deletion-unanswered`. It runs again at the next schedule or request.
