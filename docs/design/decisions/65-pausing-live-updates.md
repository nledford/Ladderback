# Decision 65: Pausing live updates

**A paused page shows a copy of itself taken at the pause while its events stream goes on, and the resume shows the live state at once and says one sentence** (recorded 2026-11-11, §4.5; #280). The Operations page, the one page that follows the daemon live, has a "Pause live updates" toggle button (WCAG 2.2.2, A3). Pressing it holds the page still: the heading, the table and every row show what they showed at the press. A banner beside the button counts the changes waiting. The announcer stops saying what happens by itself and goes on answering what the reader does. Pressing it again shows every change at once and says once how many runs ended meanwhile and how many of them failed.

Why:

- **The stream is kept, not closed.** Closing the socket at the pause and reading the history again at the resume would replay nothing twice, but the page would then miss the events of the moment between the read and the reopened stream, which is the reconnect path's job (§4.4), not the pause's. With the stream kept, its reconnects and the re-reads an end asks for go on filling the live state as they do on a page that isn't paused, and the page only shows a copy (`Pausable`, `crates/web/src/pause.rs`). The resume drops the copy: nothing is replayed, so no update is lost and no row shows twice. The page's own requests (a start, a cancel) keep waiting on the live state too.
- **The banner counts rows, not events.** A paused run reports progress many times, and "37 changes are waiting" would count updates nobody will see one by one. The banner counts each operation whose row will look different when the page resumes (a row that changed, came or went), plus one when the connection's phase changed (`OperationsState::changes_since`), so the count is what the reader will find changed.
- **One sentence at the resume, in place of one per run.** The runs that end while the page is paused are counted, not announced, and the resume says "Live updates are on again. 3 runs ended while they were paused, and one of them failed." politely, then whatever else waited. A failure that ended while paused is not said assertively: the reader asked for quiet, the failure is in the sentence and on the page, and an alert at the moment they resume would talk over the summary they asked for. The count follows the same rule as the announcements it replaces: only a status seen to change from queued or running counts, and the first sight of a row only seeds it.
- **Replies are still said.** What answers the reader's own press (a sort, "Backup started.", "Still waiting…", a form's result, a toast) is `Kind::Reply` and is said as usual, because the reader is waiting for it; only `Kind::Event`s, the things that happen by themselves, wait. A cancelled run sends no event, so the end the page sees after a Cancel is that press's only answer: it is a reply too, and not counted in the resume's sentence. The pause's own "Live updates are paused." is written at once rather than after the 2 s merge window, so a quick resume never joins it into one message; a resume before it is written drops it, even when it joined a write still to come. The events that waited are said after the resume's sentence, in one write per region, deduplicated and bounded by the same 16 as a modal's queue.
- **A toggle with a constant name, described by the banner.** The button keeps its name, "Pause live updates", and says its state with `aria-pressed`, as the APG's toggle-button pattern asks: a name that changed to "Resume" would make a screen reader say a new control under the reader's focus. The banner is a paragraph the button points at with `aria-describedby`, not a live region: the page keeps one `status` region, the announcer's, and the banner is read with the button. The button is built once and never replaced, so focus stays on it through both presses (2.4.3).
- **No pause across a reload.** Nothing is stored: leaving the page or reloading it ends the pause, and the held events are released with no sentence of their own. A pause that outlived the page would leave a reader wondering why a fresh page doesn't move.

Left out: a pause shared by every page (only Operations follows the daemon live today; a later live page adds its own control, with the same component), a pause on the Snapshots page's maintenance panel (it follows only a run the reader started there), and a pause timer.
