# Decision 114: How an entry of a snapshot is downloaded from the web UI

**A download hands one entry of one snapshot to the browser that asked: a file as itself, a folder or the snapshot's top as one tar or zip archive whose entries are stored, not compressed. It is confirmed by step-up, as a restore is, then waits behind a ticket the browser fetches once within a minute, and streams a piece at a time. At most two run at once; a third is refused, never queued. Each is recorded in the operation history as a restore is** (recorded 2026-11-16, §10.4, §4.1, §9; the archive crates, the history, the busy refusal, the refusal without random bytes and the restart's effect decided by the maintainer on #564, 2026-11-16).

**Amended 2026-11-16 (#847):** a download's row, like a restore's, names the entry it read (`entry`, data-model §9): its path from the snapshot's top as the file system wrote it, masked of the repository's secrets and at most 1 KiB, where this decision first kept no entry path.

## Why

- **The archive crates.** A folder's archive is written by `tar` 0.4 and `zip` 9.6, each with its default features off, approved by the maintainer. They bring `crc32fast`, `constant_time_eq` and `typed-path`, and on macOS and the BSDs `nix` 0.32, which `zip` 9.6 names for those systems with no feature to turn it off; it isn't built on Linux or Windows. Every license is one `deny.toml` already allows: tar (MIT OR Apache-2.0), zip (MIT), crc32fast (MIT OR Apache-2.0), constant_time_eq (CC0-1.0 OR MIT-0 OR Apache-2.0), typed-path (MIT OR Apache-2.0), nix (MIT).
- **Stored, not compressed.** Zip entries are `Stored`: compressing would need a further dependency, and most of what a backup holds compresses little or is compressed already. Compression can come later as its own dependency decision.
- **Built as it is read.** The worker reaches the entry through the snapshot's trees, as a restore does, and writes the archive while it reads: each entry's header, then its contents from rustic's dump, so no file is held whole. A zip writer still keeps each entry's name until it writes the central directory at the end. Devices, pipes and sockets have no contents and are left out.
- **A zip entry's name uses `/` only.** The zip format separates folders by `/` alone, and many Windows extractors (Explorer, 7-Zip) take `\` for one too: a Unix file named `..\x` could be written outside the extraction folder. Each `\` in a zip entry's name becomes `_`, so `a\b` and `a_b` in one folder both become `a_b`, a clash the extractor then settles. A tar entry keeps its exact name. rustic's own `dump --archive zip` keeps the `\` (rustic 0.11.4, checked on a throwaway repository); this differs from it on purpose.
- **Pieces, unmasked.** The worker hands the daemon pieces of at most 64 KiB, base64 on its event stream (87 KiB of text, under the 1 MiB message cap, decision 32), and the daemon holds one piece of each download at a time. The pieces are the entry's own bytes and are never masked: a redactor would change a file's contents, and a person may download a file that holds a secret of their own.
- **A ticket, fetched once.** `POST …/snapshots/{snapshot}/download` answers 201 with a ticket once the worker has found the entry: 32 random bytes, never logged or shown. `GET /api/v1/downloads/{ticket}` takes it once and streams the attachment, named in `Content-Disposition` by an ASCII `filename` and a UTF-8 `filename*`. A ticket nobody fetches within its minute is given up, its worker stopped, by a sweep every 10 s, or by the next download step-up confirms, which runs on a task of its own; never by the `GET`, which waits for nothing, so a browser that hangs up can't leave a download running in the history (#846). The browser saves it through `location.assign`, as any download, and the page stays.
- **Two at once, a third refused.** Each download is a worker holding the repository's index for as long as the browser reads, so at most two run at once; a third, or one beyond 16 waiting tickets, is `downloads-busy` (409). Queueing it would keep a confirmation alive for a browser that may have gone.
- **In the history, as a restore.** A download takes no I7 slot, runs no hooks and isn't queued, but it hands a snapshot's contents out of the agent, so the history keeps it as `OperationKind::Download`, triggered by hand, written by the download itself to the store. Like a restore's, its row records no entry path.
- **No random bytes, no ticket.** An agent that can't draw a ticket's random bytes refuses with `downloads-unavailable` (503, no arguments; the log says why), as a proposal does with `proposals-unavailable` (decision 89).
- **A restart.** A download doesn't hold a restart off: the stop cuts one being streamed short, and the browser fails it. While a restart waits for the running operations, a new download is refused `restart-pending`, as a restore is.
- **Listings take turns too (#710).** A listing's worker also reads the whole index, so at most two run at once, of every repository together, as folder reads do (decision 102): the wait counts against the read timeout.

Known limits: the short ID guards against a misclick only, as for a restore. A browser that can't save a download (a full disk, a blocked download) fails it on its own side; the history then shows it canceled. Downloading from the hub is M5's.
