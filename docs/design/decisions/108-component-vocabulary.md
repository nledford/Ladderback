# Decision 108: One vocabulary of tones, sizes, placements and sides

**Every shared component takes its color, size and placement from one vocabulary, `crates/web/src/variants.rs`, as it is built or reworked: the tones Primary, Secondary, Success, Warning, Error and Inherit; the sizes Small, Medium, Large and XLarge; the placements Inline, Start, Center and End. Success, Warning and Error draw in three status hues of their own, beside the one accent for action, and never carry a meaning alone** (recorded 2026-11-15, §4.5; decided by the maintainer on #798, part of #776).

**Amended 2026-11-16 (the maintainer, #778):** a component may choose its own default tone and says so in its §4.5 row: the icon draws in Inherit, the color of the text around it, unless its caller names a tone; Primary stays the default of the vocabulary and of the spinner. The sizes now have one shared scale, Small 1em, Medium 1.5em, Large 2em and XLarge 3em: each size's class sets `--size`, which a component draws its length with, so a spinner and an icon of one size line up. A component that needs other lengths may deviate, with its reason in its §4.5 row. Where "Why" and "Left out" below say each component maps the sizes to lengths of its own, this replaces it; there is still no default size in the vocabulary.

**Amended 2026-11-16 (the maintainer, #785):** the vocabulary gains `Side`, where a part that floats beside a control stands (the tooltip first, later the popover, #784): Above (the default), Below, Start and End, the last two in the page's direction. A part with no room on its side flips to the opposite one, then to the other axis, and stays inside the viewport. It is not a Placement, which aligns a part in its own line, and it has no class: the page measures the control and places the part with one host-tested rule (`components/tooltip/rules.rs`). The tooltip takes a side and no tone or size: a note, not a status, its text following its control's.

**Amended 2026-11-16 (#792):** not every shared component takes the whole vocabulary. A component takes a tone and a size only where they mean something, and its §4.5 row says why it takes neither: the step-up field, the tooltip, the table and the tabs take none (§4.5, "The shared vocabulary").

**Amended 2026-11-17 (the maintainer, decision 117):** a tone, a size and a placement become sets of Tailwind utilities that `variants.rs` returns, as each component moves onto utilities (#931); until then each stays a class of `style.css`. Where "Why" below says each value is a class of `style.css`, this replaces it. The values, their meanings and the shared size scale don't change.

**Amended 2026-11-17 (#941):** each status tone gains a pair of colors for a part tinted in it, its words on its fill: `--success-fg` on `--success-bg`, `--warning-fg` on `--warning-bg` and `--error-fg` on `--error-bg`, a pale fill under dark words in light colors and a deep fill under pale words in dark ones, each mapped as a Tailwind color.

`variants.rs` gives them by tone (`StatusTone`, whose `pair()` is the two utilities), so the badge, the alert, the banner, the toast and the progress bar take one pair rather than each inventing its own; the badge-only `--badge-success-*` and `--badge-error-*` go, and the error words also draw a form's errors on the page. The contrast gate holds each pair's words to 4.5:1 on its fill in both schemes. Where "Why" below says the badge pairs keep their own text tokens, this replaces it for the three status tones; Loading and Empty keep the badge's own pairs.

The measure that keeps Warning and Error apart: their fills differ by at least 0.1 in OKLab lightness (0 black, 1 white) in each scheme, and the gate fails a pair closer than that. Lightness, not hue: amber and red are neighboring hues, which a reader who can't tell red from green sees as one, and a dark fill holds too little color for its hue to tell; lightness survives both. A tenth of the scale is the floor because #790's dark fills, 0.07 apart (1.24:1 in luminance), read as one. The values chosen put Error's fill further from the page than Warning's in both schemes, darker in light and lighter in dark, so the failure is the more visible of the two; the gate measures the distance, not its direction.

Success is not held to the measure: #941 asked that Warning and Error be told apart, though the same argument reaches green. In light colors Success's fill sits between the other two, 0.025 from Warning's in lightness, so a Success part beside a Warning one can read alike; holding it apart too means retuning Success's fill, a choice for the first component that sets the two side by side. The toast (#789), first named for it, shows one toast at a time and so never does. Until then, as for every tone, a part's words and its icon tell its status, never its fill alone (1.4.1).

**Amended 2026-11-17 (the maintainer, #794):** the progress bar takes no status pair. A pair is for words on a tinted fill, and the bar's fill carries no words, so the bar fills with its tone's own token (`--accent`, `--muted`, `--success`, `--warning` or `--error`, or the text's color for Inherit) on its Ledger Gray track (`--surface`). The fill is a part, not text, so the contrast gate holds it to 3:1 (1.4.11) against the track and against the page, for each tone in both schemes. The step and the percentage keep the text's color, so a bar's tone never tells how a run goes alone (1.4.1). Where the #941 amendment above names the progress bar among the parts that take one pair, this replaces it.

## Why

- **One set of words for every component.** The shared component set (#776) grows by some twenty components. Without one vocabulary each would name its colors, sizes and sides its own way, and a page would learn each one. Each value is a class of `style.css`, so a component names a value of the module and never a color, a length or a side.
- **Tones.** Primary is Ladder Blue (`--accent`), the default; Secondary is Slate Note (`--muted`); Inherit is `currentColor`, the color of the text around a part inside another component, such as a spinner in a button. There is no Info tone: Primary covers it. A component's §4.5 row names any tone it leaves out because it makes no sense there.
- **Three status hues, never alone.** Tick Green (`--success`), Flag Amber (`--warning`) and Red Ink (`--error`) are real, recognisable hues, each at 4.5:1 or more on `--bg` and `--surface` in light and dark, every pair in `scripts/check-contrast.ts`. DESIGN.md's One Blue Rule had allowed one accent and no other hue; it now allows one accent for action plus these three, which a part always pairs with words or an icon (1.4.1, A2). Under forced colors each is `CanvasText`, so the words tell the tones apart. The badge pairs keep their own text tokens.
- **Sizes carry no numbers.** A small spinner and a small button share no length, so each component maps the four sizes to `em` lengths of its own, written in its §4.5 row, and a size grows with the reader's text (1.4.4). For the same reason there is no default size.
- **Placements in logical terms.** Inline (the default) keeps a part in its line of text; Start, Center and End put it on a line of its own, at its start, middle or end in the page's direction, so Start is the right in a right-to-left page (§8.4). The class goes on the line, an element of the component's own around its part, never on the part's root: it sets the line's `display`, which a badge's own would override and which would stretch a button across the line.

## Left out

- **Info, and a fourth status hue.** Primary covers information; a new meaning still gets words, weight or an icon (DESIGN.md, Don'ts).
- **A default size.** Each component picks its own.
