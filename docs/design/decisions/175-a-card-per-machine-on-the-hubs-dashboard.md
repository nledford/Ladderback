# Decision 175: The hub's dashboard is a card per machine, the hub's own first, and each machine has a page of its own

**On a running hub, the dashboard ([decision 173](173-a-hubs-start-page-is-its-dashboard.md)) says in one sentence how many machines it shows and links each that needs attention, then shows a flat card per machine: the hub's own first, marked "This hub", then each approved machine in the order it was approved. A card tells the machine's hostname as its heading, why it needs attention when it does, whether it is online now or when it last connected, its status in a badge, the progress of the operation it runs, then its last backup, its next run and how many plans it has. Its controls are a link to the machine's page, `/machines/<machine-id>`, which holds what the machine's section on the dashboard held, and on the hub's own card a Back up menu of its plans** (recorded 2026-11-27, §4.4, §4.5, #1675; the maintainer's decision).

## Why

A tester of 0.6.0 found two faults in the hub's dashboard. It showed the machines paired with the hub, but not the hub's own machine, which runs backups too. And each machine was a full section (its connection, its progress, its 10 latest operations and its plans), so with a few machines, telling whether each was backed up took scrolling past every one. The dashboard's question is whether each machine is backed up, and a card per machine answers it at a glance; a machine's detail moves to a page of its own, reached from its card.

## Decided

1. **The sentence.** Under the heading and the start page's notices, the dashboard's introduction and "Pause live updates", one sentence counts the machines shown and those that need attention ("3 machines. One needs attention:"), then a list links to the page of each that does, saying why ("The last run of photos on demo-laptop failed."). It is the at-a-glance answer in words, which a screen reader hears before any card.
2. **One card per machine.** The hub's own card comes first, with "This hub" in words under its hostname, then each approved machine's, in the order the hub approved them. Cards don't reorder as their machines change: a card that moved while the reader read it, or held focus in it, would lose their place. The hub's own card reads the hub itself: who it is from `GET /api/v1/machine`, its plans from `GET /api/v1/status`, read again on opening and each time one of its operations comes or changes its status, and its operations as the Operations page follows them.
3. **What a card tells.** After the heading, the attention mark when the machine needs it; "Online now." or when it last connected ([decision 159](159-long-lived-hub-connections.md)); the shared status badge: Running while an operation runs, else how its latest run that ended came out, else "Not run yet"; the shared progress bar while an operation runs, never announced (decision 83). Then a description list: **Last backup**, when the latest run that ended did, or "None yet"; **Next run**, the earliest of its plans' next runs, "Was due …" once that run is past due, or "None planned"; **Plans**, how many it has, those it left out of its report among them, or "Not reported yet". Each time reads in the viewer's zone, and in the machine's beside it when the two differ (#115).
4. **Needs attention, the first reason that holds.** The machine is offline, and its next planned run is more than 5 minutes past; a plan's last run failed; or its next planned run is more than 5 minutes past while nothing runs. The 5 minutes allow for a machine that sends its plans only when they change, and for clocks a few minutes apart. The mark is the card's first paragraph after its heading: a Warning icon a screen reader doesn't hear, "Needs attention:" in bold, then the reason's sentence. Never color alone, and never a colored edge or fill: a failure is stated once (DESIGN.md).
5. **Two controls, never a clickable card.** The card is an `<article>` named by its heading, and neither it nor its heading is a stop for Tab. Its controls come after its facts: on the hub's own card, **Back up ⁨hostname⁩**, the shared `DisclosureMenu` with a row "Start backup of ⁨plan⁩" for each of its plans, which sends the Operations page's request and says "Backup started." politely; then on every card the link **Open ⁨hostname⁩** to the machine's page. Pressing a row's button closes the menu and returns focus to its button, as the disclosure menu now does for any row's button.
6. **The machine's page.** `/machines/<machine-id>` is headed by the machine's hostname and titled "⁨hostname⁩ – Ladderback" once the page has read it. It shows what the machine's section on the dashboard showed: online now or when it last connected, a warning when the hub let go of its oldest reports, the progress bar, the table of its latest operations and the table of its plans. The hub's own page says "This hub" and that it shows the hub's own plans and operations. It follows the hub's events, and "Pause live updates" holds it still, as the dashboard does (A3, decision 65). For a machine the hub hasn't approved, and on a daemon that isn't a hub, it is a page not found.
7. **The grid, under The Summary Card Rule.** The cards stand in a list laid out as a grid whose columns are at least 18rem wide, or the page's width where it is narrower, so it wraps by its own width: one column at 320 px with 200 % text, several side by side on a wide page (The 320 Rule). Each card is flat: the page's color inside a 1px edge in Field Edge with the small corner, and no shadow (The Flat Ledger Rule). It is the shared `MachineCard` (§4.5), in Tailwind utilities with logical properties only. The card follows The Summary Card Rule in DESIGN.md, which states when a card is the right shape and how it looks, so a later card is a decision under that rule rather than an exception.
8. **Who the machine is.** `GET /api/v1/machine` tells, beside its standing, the machine ID the daemon runs under, its hostname, and its IANA time zone, each left out when the daemon has none to tell (§4.2). A hostname a hub would refuse from a machine that pairs ([decision 120](120-agent-hub-connection-security.md), "Hostnames") is left out too, so the hub's own card is never named by a name no paired machine could have.

## Considered and rejected

- **A clickable card**, the whole card one link. Its name would be the card's every word, and the Back up menu inside it would be a control within a link, which neither a screen reader nor the keyboard can reach as itself. Two controls, each named by the machine it acts on, say what each does.
- **A Back up menu shown unavailable on a paired machine's card** until the hub can ask a machine to back up (#1464). A control the reader can't use only says what the page can't do yet; the menu comes to those cards when it works.
- **The machines that need attention first.** The order would change as their machines change, under the reader's eyes and focus; the sentence names those that need attention first instead.
- **The hub's own plans table below the machines**, as decision 173 had rejected it. The hub's own card is the same kind of summary as every other card, of a machine like theirs, so the page stays one summary; the hub's plans and operations in full are on its page, as each machine's are on its own.

## Known limits

- A paired machine's card has no Back up menu until #1464 lands.
- A hub whose hostname a hub would refuse from a pairing machine, or whose `GET /api/v1/machine` names no machine ID, shows no card of its own: the sentence and the cards are then the approved machines', as before this decision.
- Whether a run is past due is judged by the viewer's clock, read again every minute, against the machine's planned time; a clock more than 5 minutes off marks a machine too early or too late.
- A card's last backup and its status come from the plans the machine last reported, so a machine that never reported its plans shows "None yet" and "Not run yet", whatever its operations say.
