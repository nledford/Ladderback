# Decision 45: Masking a text cut at a cap

**A text cut at a cap is masked as a cut text (`Redactor::redact_cut`): every whole secret as anywhere else, and the end of what was kept from where it begins any form of a secret, with the bytes of a character the cut went through left out; a text another side cut and marked with `…` is masked so up to the mark, which stays unless it ends a whole secret (`Redactor::redact_clipped`)** (recorded 2026-11-10, §10.2, I4; #158). The places that cut and then mask: a secret command's stderr (8 KiB kept; masked as cut when it fills them), a hook's stdout and stderr (64 KiB each, when the truncated flag is set), a worker's stderr line cut at the message cap, the text of a worker's events (prefix, title, item, detail, log message, failure detail) and a command's detail in a refusal (both marked `…`). The worker's own events are masked before it cuts them, but without the proxy credentials of its environment, which only the daemon masks; the daemon masks any event text that ends in `…` as cut text, which also covers any other secret the worker doesn't know. A tail that only looks like the start of a secret is masked too: more is masked than needs be, never less. Text that is masked first and cut after (`Problem::command_failed`'s 512 bytes, the app's fakes) needs nothing more. Rejected: dropping as many bytes of a cut text as the longest secret has, less one, before masking (the rule this replaces), because it also cut a whole secret that ended within those bytes and left its start behind; and giving the worker the proxy credentials to mask before its cut, because the daemon-side rule covers them and every other secret the worker lacks in one place. A stream cut by a timeout rather than a cap is #159.
