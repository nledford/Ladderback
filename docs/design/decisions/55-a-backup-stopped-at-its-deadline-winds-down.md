# Decision 55: A backup stopped at its deadline winds down

**A backup still archiving at its deadline winds down as a cancelled one does (decision 50): no snapshot write starts once the worker notices the deadline, the index of every pack it wrote is saved, and the operation fails `deadline-passed`** (recorded 2026-11-11, §4.1; #209). Before, the worker's watchdog called `process::exit(75)` at once and the daemon killed the worker with no grace, so the packs written since rustic's last index save were left with no index listing them: `rustic check` warned about each until a prune removed them, and the next backup saved the same data again.

Decided:

1. When the watchdog notices the deadline, it first asks for the worker's cancel, with the deadline as its cause, under the same lock as SIGTERM's. From then on the backend that takes a snapshot first refuses one (I3), so a snapshot write either was admitted before that or never starts. The watchdog waits for the time left to the deadline, but at most `watchdog_interval` (5 s by default) at a time, and its waits don't count the time the machine is suspended: it notices the deadline when it comes, and one that passed during a suspend within one interval of waking. A snapshot write that starts in that gap is not refused; the gap predates this decision, the worker then went on until the watchdog's exit, and the prune margin covers it. Only then does the watchdog write `Failed { DeadlinePassed }` as the stream's last line. It tries the stream once; when another line was being written then, the job writes the failure as the worker ends.
2. A backup that is archiving winds down: its source reads nothing more, rustic saves the index of its packs, and the job, not the watchdog, ends the worker with 75 once rustic returns. The watchdog waits for that 30 s at most, then exits with 75, which bounds a worker whose daemon is gone. Any other job ends at once with 75, as before. The first cause stands: a backup wound down by a SIGTERM still ends with 143 if its deadline passes meanwhile. One wound down by its deadline ends with 75, but a SIGTERM that lands after rustic returned and before the worker exited ends it with 143 at once; when that SIGTERM is the daemon's stop at the deadline, the operation is recorded `deadline-passed` all the same (item 4).
3. A snapshot write that began before the deadline finishes, in both halves of a hot/cold repository. I3 exempts it, and the prune margin covers it; ending the worker during it could leave the snapshot in the hot half alone, which decision 50 rules out for a cancel. The operation is still `deadline-passed`, and in that case its snapshot exists, as after a cancel that comes once the snapshot was written.
4. The daemon stops the job at the deadline with its grace period (SIGTERM, then SIGKILL), rather than dropping it, whether its own timer or the worker's report of the deadline comes first, and records the operation `deadline-passed` once the worker has exited, whatever its exit code. So the operation's end time can be up to the grace period past the deadline, as a cancelled operation's is past the cancel.

Repository jobs keep their own deadline and its kill (decision 36): none of them archives.

Rejected: ending the worker at once when the deadline comes during the snapshot write, which could leave the snapshot in one half of a hot/cold repository.
