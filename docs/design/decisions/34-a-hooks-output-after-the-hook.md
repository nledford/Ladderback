# Decision 34: A hook's output after the hook has ended

**A hook is reaped when it ends; the runner reads its output for one more second and then lets go of the pipes, whatever still holds them** (recorded 2026-11-10, §6.1; #110). A process a hook started (a daemon, a background job) holds the write end of the hook's stdout and stderr for as long as it runs, so waiting for the end of the pipes before waiting for the hook reported a hook that had ended as timed out at its timeout. Decided: the hook is waited for, the pipes are read alongside, and after the exit they are read on for 1 s (`PIPE_GRACE`) for what the hook left in them and what the process that holds them says as it starts, then dropped. A process that writes to them afterwards gets SIGPIPE, or EPIPE where it ignores that; nothing is signalled, as the hook was reaped. Why: a pipe nobody reads blocks its writer once it is full, so the choices were to drop the read end, which fails the descendant's write, or to read for as long as the descendant lives, which holds a task and a file descriptor for a daemon's lifetime and keeps its log in a field no one reads; the password-command runner already lets go of its pipes after a second. A hook that starts something long-lived redirects its output.
