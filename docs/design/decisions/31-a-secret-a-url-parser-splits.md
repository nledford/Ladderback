# Decision 31: A secret a URL parser splits

**A piece of a secret is a needle of its own from 8 bytes; shorter ones are masked only as part of the whole secret** (recorded 2026-11-10, §10.2). A parser splits a secret at `@`, `#`, `?`, `/` and `:`, and a library may print the parts one by one; each piece of at least 8 bytes (the first 64 of a secret) is masked in every form the secret is, and the whole secret is also masked as the parser prints it across parts (a host in lower case, a path as it is). Why: a floor of 8 bytes is what keeps the pieces from masking ordinary text (the same floor as a line of a secret that spans lines); a shorter piece of a secret that also holds long ones leaks only a few characters, and in the whole URL it is masked with the rest. Not covered: a non-ASCII host (needs an IDNA library in the domain), percent-decoding in a host and dot segments in a path.
